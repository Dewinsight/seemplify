# Initial role configuration

Status: proposed examples, pending target organisation and lawful SFIA source confirmation. No employee rating or decision is seeded.

Use one published quarterly competency cycle containing both roles. Initial anchor: 1 July–30 September 2026. Later periods keep separate appraisals, source definitions and results. Managers start each completed period; this does not automatically assign employees or send invitations.

| Role | Proposed native expectations | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Solution Architect | ARCH 5, REQM 4, RLMT 5, FEAS 4, MEAS 4 | Architecture decisions and alternatives; traceable requirements; stakeholder agreements; cost/risk feasibility; measured production outcomes |
| Product Manager | PROD 5, REQM 4, RLMT 5, FEAS 4, MEAS 4 | Outcome-based roadmap decisions; validated needs and acceptance criteria; priority negotiations; experiments and business cases; adoption and outcome measurements |

These target levels are proposed starting points, not SFIA-prescribed job definitions. Each organisation must own its role expectations. Skill-native level ranges must come from the published framework version; do not create ARCH levels 1–3 or 7.

## Authored responsibilities

**Solution Architect**

1. Document solution boundaries, alternatives, dependencies and decision trade-offs.
2. Trace functional and non-functional requirements through delivery and verification.
3. Align stakeholders on architecture decisions and communicate unresolved risks.
4. Evaluate security, operability, cost and implementation feasibility.
5. Measure whether the delivered solution achieves its intended outcomes.

**Product Manager**

1. Define measurable user and business outcomes and maintain a prioritised roadmap.
2. Turn validated needs into clear requirements and acceptance criteria.
3. Negotiate priorities and dependencies with delivery teams and stakeholders.
4. Test assumptions, options, feasibility and expected value before major investment.
5. Use adoption, quality and outcome evidence to adjust product decisions.

## Assessment policy

Keep observed native levels and expected levels visible. Any numeric normalization is an explicit cycle policy, separate from the human appraisal rating. The AI asks for concrete work, decisions and evidence for each criterion; it cannot invent evidence, award SFIA certification or decide a final employment outcome. Missing required evidence blocks publication unless an authorised assessor records an exception.
