# Performance reliability: product stories and acceptance plan

20 October 2026 · Initial review baseline: `f846ef54`; tested source: `069a20b01f21741400484639f257dd043aea3ea0`; released main: `da2e03c3c1c90c0297c7539abbc642d58a823264` (PR #147).

## 1. Outcome and scope

Users must be able to set goals, start an authorized appraisal, complete each handoff, and find the resulting history without needing console diagnostics or help from an administrator. Every available feature needs script and browser coverage across its permitted users, including denial and recovery paths. A page loading is insufficient evidence that its main action works.

This is an acceptance addendum for the existing Performance product. It does not replace the PRD or the completed [competency programme](../../../docs/competency-assessment/IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md). That programme already contains product stories, architecture, design concepts, role skills, QA and illustrated reporting. Reuse its contracts and evidence rather than creating a competing assessment model.

The source review found subsequent repairs beyond the original 17 October audit: lifecycle removal/restoration, canonical colleague lookup, native-call relay recovery, draft preservation, role-only launch/scoring, reviewer scope, AI-off policy/status and embedded-report retry. Relevant historical source commits: `6b8210c2`, `7d688175`, `d3cfe8d3`, `e26c3d88`, `a430b591`, `4f4ee4a3`. Current test, release and provider proof are recorded separately below; a historical repair commit alone is insufficient evidence.

## 2. Product method and handoffs

Stories specify actor, need, outcome and observable acceptance. They link a user complaint to evidence. This follows the [GOV.UK user-story guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/writing-user-stories). Existing reports and current source inform the baseline; assumptions about unobserved users remain assumptions, following [GOV.UK user-research guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs). These are primary delivery references, not authority for Seemplify's employment policies.

Each story moves independently through:

`PM: need + acceptance → architect: contracts + risks → UX: screen/state review → developer: bounded fix → QA: replay → PM + architect: acceptance → release verification`

PM can prepare the next story while architecture/design reviews the previous one. Assign shared-file ownership before concurrent implementation. Retain a PM and architect for verification; the architect may perform QA, with a separate reviewer for their own changes. Staffing and the overall worker ceiling belong in the coordinator's execution plan; this document does not create another team or backlog.

## 3. Personas and authority

Exercise employee, teamless employee, team lead, line manager, department head, HR manager, organization administrator and owner. Include a peer, an unselected manager, a foreign organization, anonymous access and a changed/revoked permission. Role labels alone do not authorize an action: use current signed Identity permissions, active organization, scope and selected-reviewer rules.

Organization-wide OKRs normally belong to authorized HR/admin/owner users. An ordinary manager must not acquire organization-wide authority merely because they can review their staff. Explicit central grants and denies remain authoritative. A manager's ability to launch an appraisal is distinct from the ability to administer all company cycles.

### Accepted storage-change handoff

REL-13 is an authorized implementation priority, not an optional investigation. The architect owns central provider selection and provider-aware metadata/delivery contracts; the coordinator owns configuration and release verification. Credentials remain in the existing secret mechanism, and generated delivery links must not appear in public artifacts or logs. A stored permanent public URL is not an acceptable substitute for authorized private delivery.

QA must retain separate evidence for: (1) scripted real-provider TXT/PDF/DOCX upload → authenticated download → byte comparison → exact synthetic-object cleanup; (2) browser attachment save, reload and download; (3) anonymous, cross-tenant, expired-link and insufficient-permission denial; (4) upload failure/retry without a false success or unrelated deletion; and (5) old Azure-reference and private competency-file compatibility. No bulk migration, old-object deletion or change to another product's storage selection is included. Current provider round trips must pass before declaring this story complete.
